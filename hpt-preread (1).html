Before You Begin

This isn't a training. It's a journey.

High-Performing Teams (HPT) is not a class or a one-time program — it's an ongoing journey where your team builds habits around what you collaborate on and how you collaborate. Teams succeed by practicing these habits, not by attending a session once.

HPTs focus on the internal dynamics of a team — collaborating more intentionally and effectively. Success and high performance are the outcome of that collaboration, not the starting point. This pre-read exists to get you and your team ready to do that work together.

Step into your Learning Zone

Growth doesn't happen in the Comfort Zone. This journey will ask you and your team to deliberately stretch beyond what's familiar — without tipping into panic.

Death Zone
Panic Zone
Learning Zone
Comfort Zone the sum of our current habits

Too little stretch, and habits never change. Too much, and the team shuts down. The Learning Zone is where real habit-building happens.

How to use this pre-read

The workshop is built around a single model: the Six Habits of High-Performing Teams. They're not a checklist — they're a continuous cycle your team keeps returning to, all circling one question: how do we learn? Tap any habit below to jump straight to it.

Habit 1 of 6

Clarify Context

Before a team can agree on its purpose, roles, or ways of working, it needs a shared picture of the environment it's operating in. This habit builds that shared understanding first — everything else is built on top of it.

Build a shared understanding of where we are, what's changing around us, and why the team exists now.

Two things to clarify, together

1

Identify the factors that impact your team's performance

Think broadly: organizational change, market conditions, shifting strategic priorities, resourcing constraints, stakeholder expectations, recent wins or setbacks. What's actually shaping the conditions your team works in right now?

2

Clarify how each factor impacts the team

For each factor, get specific: does it change priorities? Add pressure? Create ambiguity about who owns what? Naming the mechanism — not just the factor — is what turns context into something the team can act on.

Discuss in groups, during the workshop

What factors impact your team's performance?

How do these factors impact your team?

💡
Why this comes first

A team that skips this habit ends up debating its purpose or roles without a shared read on reality — which usually surfaces as disagreement about something else entirely. Clarifying context first removes that noise.

Habit 2 of 6

Define Your "Why"

Agree on the unique contribution this team must make — what you must do together that no one else can. Two steps get you there.

Two steps to your team's "Why"

1

What can only this team accomplish?

What are the things that ONLY OUR TEAM can accomplish — that nobody else can? Only for this team, the people in this room. Capture these as verbs: things the team does.

2

What impact do you want to see?

If the team does those things successfully, what's the impact — the thing you'll actually see happen as a result?

Habit 3 of 6

Set Clear Roles

Who works with whom, on what? Very little of a team's work is actually shared by the entire team — most of it happens in subgroups or by individuals. This habit makes that visible, then assigns clear roles to it.

The "Radar Screen" — most work lives in the outer rings, not the center.

Three steps

1

Identify the projects

List every project or initiative that involves at least one other team member — one initiative per item. Don't include business-as-usual work. Note the owner for each.

2

Sort them on the Radar Screen

As a group, remove duplicates and sort each item into one of three rings: Team Work (everyone involved), Subgroup (some members), or Individual (one person only). Discuss and agree together.

3

Assign RACI to team and subgroup work

For everything that landed in the Team or Subgroup rings, assign clear roles using RACI (below).

Habit 4 of 6

Cultivate Collaboration

Build trust, understand each other's styles and strengths, and find ways to bring the best out of each other. This habit has two parts: understanding people, and understanding the collaboration itself.

Part A · Understanding People

4.1 — Understand Yourself: DISC

DISC describes how you show up, not who you are. Everyone has all four styles to some degree — think of them like paints on an artist's palette. Two axes define the four quadrants: how you react to the world (introversion ↔ extraversion), and how you make decisions (thinking ↔ feeling).

Thinking (task-driven)
Feeling (people-driven)
Introversion
Extraversion

Tap a quadrant for details.

📄
Understanding your Extended DISC Profile

Profile I — Perceived Need to Adjust: your conscious persona right now, like a formal portrait. It's transient and shifts with context or pressure.

Profile II — Natural Style: your default behavior when relaxed, like a candid photo. It's more stable — strengths above the midpoint take less energy; areas below require you to stretch.

The midpoint represents the average person. There's no right or wrong relation of styles — most people show two or three styles as strengths, not just one. Focus on the ~80% that feels true, and ask others to help you see your blind spots.

4.2 — Understand Others

You can pick up real signals about someone's style before you ever see their profile: their tone of voice and word choice, body language and gestures, how they interact with you and at what pace, and even their written communication.

The same underlying trait reads as a strength or a flaw depending on the situation — and who's looking at it. That's exactly why naming it explicitly matters more than judging it.

4.3 — How to Work Together

Once you know your own style, turn it into something usable: 3–5 concrete expectations for how others should collaborate with you. Everyone shares theirs, so the whole team knows how to work with each person.

Part B · Not All Collaboration Is Equal

The Comparative Value Contribution scale

Collaboration exists on a spectrum — and more of it isn't automatically better. The goal is to increase the intentionality of collaboration, but only collaborate when and where it's actually needed.

Intentional Collaboration
Planned upfront, clear intersection — highest value creation
Reactive Collaboration

Clarify expectations with "Behavioral Contracting"

Set expectations for how the team will collaborate — using specific, observable behaviors (verbs), not generic "team norms." Base it on this team, this work, and who you actually are as individuals.

Habit 5 of 6

Align Our Ways of Working

Agree on how we communicate, make decisions, resolve conflicts, and hold effective meetings. Four areas of guiding questions help your team build these agreements together.

🗓 Team Meetings

  • What types of topics are worth meeting for?
  • How do we set and share the agenda before meetings?
  • How do we keep meetings focused and on time?
  • How do we encourage participation from everyone?
  • How do we capture and share meeting notes and action items?
  • How do we follow up on decisions and next steps?
✎ Fillable

💬 Team Communication

  • Which channel do we use by default for quick alignment, complex topics, decisions, and FYIs?
  • What are our standard response times for normal vs. urgent items?
  • How do we ensure shared understanding?
  • How much communication is too much?
  • Where do we keep final decisions, drafts, and working files? What are our naming/versioning rules?
✎ Fillable

🎯 Team Decision-Making

  • Which decision modes do we use (consensus, voting, authority-based), and when?
  • Who is accountable for each decision?
  • Do we tend to decide too quickly or too slowly, and in which scenarios?
  • What information and risks do we consider before deciding?
  • How transparent is our decision process today? How are decisions communicated?
  • What causes frustrations today?
✎ Fillable

⚡ Addressing Team Conflict

  • Is everyone comfortable speaking up about concerns and disagreements?
  • What types of conflict does the team face most often — personality-driven (emotional) or substantive (task-related)?
  • How do we show empathy and respect, even when we disagree?
  • How do we resolve conflicts as a team?
  • How can we use conflicts as opportunities for team learning and improvement?

Habit 6 of 6

Review, Reflect and Recommit

The last habit closes the loop. It focuses on two things: creating accountability for our commitments, and instilling a climate of continuous learning and growth.

Discuss in groups

How will we, as a team, hold ourselves accountable for our commitments?

How will we ensure we evaluate the effectiveness of our team charter and the six habits?

How will we continue to learn and grow together?

How will we celebrate successes and learn from setbacks?

Capture your aligned thoughts as the top five "ground rules" you'll use to hold one another accountable.

Formal Habits

  • Start of meetings: review the team charter
  • End of meetings: rate meeting effectiveness
  • Monthly: recognize and celebrate successes
  • Each quarter: review and update charter and RACI
  • Every six months: give 1:1 feedback, update charter
  • Onboard new joiners; get to know their preferences

Ongoing Habits

  • Hold yourself accountable for commitments and expectations
  • Ask for regular feedback
  • Hold others accountable for commitments and expectations
  • Regularly offer the gift of feedback

Choose fewer, but more impactful habits — which of these would most help your team?

Suggested next steps to "jump start" your team's learning

1

Within one month

Set collaboration commitments for each key initiative.

2

Within two months

Team reviews the charter — what's working, what isn't.

3

Within six months

Team gives each other feedback on collaboration commitments and expectations.
